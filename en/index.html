Nikita Bazooka Tattoo

I approach tattooing as a form of visual writing. The main directions of my work are lettering, calligraphy and abstract graphics. What interests me in them is the gesture of the line, the rhythm of the sign and the energy between emptiness and form.

Sacraglyph is the name of my visual language. Through it I explore symbol, ornament and the structure of writing, turning them into compositions that live on the body as naturally as on paper or canvas.

For me, what matters is not only the tattoo itself, but how it interacts with the body. I work with line direction, scale and the plasticity of form so the tattoo looks cohesive and keeps its expressiveness over time.

Each piece is created individually. I pay close attention to the idea and search for the exact composition and form that honestly reflects the person.

If this aesthetic resonates with you, message me and we can discuss your future piece.

Styles and approach

The main directions of my work are lettering, calligraphy, blackwork, graphic style and freehand.

Lettering and calligraphy

I write calligraphy and lettering in my unique SACRAGLYPH style, and also create different stylizations around the idea, composition and body anatomy.

Blackwork and graphic style

Dense saturated blackwork with minimal skin trauma: high-contrast forms, graphic structures and compositions that stay cohesive and expressive on the body.

Freehand

All works are built around body anatomy: the lines and form are drawn so the tattoo naturally fits the exact placement.

How booking works

A quick overview of how a tattoo project starts.

1. Idea

Everything starts with the idea. I need to understand what you want to make, where it should be placed, what size you expect and what vibe the tattoo should carry. You can also attach reference images that you visually like.

2. Discussion

We meet in the studio for a free consultation and discuss the sketch in more detail, take measurements, select references and plan the budget. Remote consultation is also possible.

3. Session

By the session day, I prepare the composition: during the design process I send intermediate results and discuss changes if they are needed and make sense. Then I do the tattoo in a comfortable studio setting, with breaks when needed. Numbing is also possible.
